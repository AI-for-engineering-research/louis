Your interpretation: right mechanism, wrong dominant cause

The land pixels are at 8 µm, and coarsening them would lower G — the direction is sound. But:

1. 89% of those pixels are sim_only. Reference C13 there is 280.9 K, sim C14 is only 3.6 K colder than the reference. ERA5 has put a 155 g/m² warm liquid deck over Florida that GOES doesn't register as cold cloud at all. By this repo's own rule (RESEARCH-LOOP #5, and the settled sim_only finding), that's cloud-placement/amount error, not microphysics. Coarsening the droplets would dim a cloud that shouldn't be that thick in the first place — cosmetically better, physically a wash.

2. The genuinely wrong pixels are over ocean, already at 14 µm. The 46 cloud_both pixels have land=0.00, r_e=14, and the worst dG in the box (+4.76; reference G there is −1.52, a clean liquid signature, and the sim gives +3.2). A land→ocean fix does nothing for these. They corroborate the existing thread-L residual: 14 µm is the low edge of the [14, 23] zero-crossing bracket, truth nearer ~20.

3. Scene-wide there is no land-vs-ocean signature at all. Warm liquid cloud over land (n=1282, r_e 8.2) has dG +0.88; over ocean (n=7225, r_e 14.0) dG +1.10. If the 8 µm continental value were systematically too fine, land should sit clearly above ocean. It doesn't. The worst-behaved liquid stratum is the partial T-ramp (r_e 8–11 µm, n=719, T_liq 271 K, LWP 58): dG +2.45 — supercooled continental cloud caught mid-ramp, not warm Florida cloud.

One thing your reading does land on: our plugin deviates from CAM here, and it's flagged in the docstring. CAM's landm is a deliberately smoothed land fraction — the CAM authors put that smoothing in for exactly your reason (airmass ≠ underlying surface at coastlines). We use local land_cov, which is 1.00 across the Florida peninsula. Restoring the smoothing is a faithfulness fix, not a tune, and it targets your mechanism precisely. It would take those 218 land pixels from 8 µm to maybe 11. Small scene-wide, but principled.

"Use the clean radius everywhere" — I'd argue against

The magnitude doesn't support 14. The trend is real (eastern-US AOD is down roughly 40–50% since the early 2000s; SO₂ emissions far more), but r_e responds through two sublinear steps: N_d ∝ AOD^0.3–0.7, then r_e ∝ N_d^(−1/3). Even the linear-N_d upper bound gives r_e × ~1.22, i.e. 8 → ~9.8 µm. A realistic exponent gives ~8.9. The pollution argument supports 8→9–10, not 8→14. (Those AOD/emission figures are from memory — worth verifying before they go in the log.)

The land–ocean r_e contrast isn't purely anthropogenic. It survives in pristine continental air: stronger continental updrafts activate more of the CCN spectrum, and biogenic/dust/biomass CCN don't clean up when SO₂ does. Kiehl's 8/14 was never a "polluted/clean" pair — it's a continental/maritime pair bundling CCN, updraft, and cloud-depth differences. Stripping the anthropogenic component doesn't get you to maritime.

It stops being CAM3. It becomes CONSTLIQ14 — an unpublished constant picked to fix a visual complaint, in a scheme whose whole selling point in the docstring is "all constants are the published CAM values; nothing is fitted." And it's already bracketed by the CONSTLIQ4/CONSTLIQ30 runs. Worth running as an ablation; not worth calling "modernized CAM3."

There's counter-evidence in the error budget. CAM3LIQ already overshoots coarse where it was fine: cloud-over-snow dG flipped +0.29 → −0.48, independently corroborated at 3.9 µm (SW −0.50 → +1.15, night-valid). Pushing every continental cloud to 14 extends that overshoot to the ~1900 land liquid pixels currently sitting at a healthy +0.88.

The bigger critique, which I think matters more

CAM3's LWC-independence is not a feature — it's what makes the scheme physically indefensible as a parametrization. A droplet radius that ignores the water content is a radiation-code convenience tuned so CAM's global shortwave cloud forcing balances, not a retrieval.

Which means the CAM3 win here is at least partly right answer, wrong reason: your own LOG says it works because it's immune to ERA5's low in-cloud LWC, after every (LWC/N_d)^⅓ scheme (Martin, MARTIN49, CAM6/MG2) landed within ±0.1 K of each other. That's a scheme succeeding by ignoring a biased input. Stacking a further tune on top ("clean everywhere") compounds the compensation — you'd be fitting a droplet radius to absorb an ERA5 liquid-water error, and the fit won't transfer to another scene.

What I'd do instead, ranked

  1. Restore CAM's landm smoothing — faithfulness fix, free, targets your Florida mechanism honestly.
  2. Test 20 µm over ocean, if a published marine deck r_e supports it — that's where the real cloud_both residual is (dG +4.76 at 14 µm), and thread L already says truth is between 14 and 30.
  3. Leave the Florida land green alone — it's the ERA5 warm-low-cloud placement problem wearing a microphysics costume. Note also that the reference has G=+1.48 over Florida land at 281 K: that's the quartz 8.5 µm emissivity dip in sandy soil, and our clear-land dG of −1.15 says IGBP under-represents it. Different error, same colour.

What landm actually is

Verified verbatim from the MPAS copy of CAM's reltab (module_ra_cam_support.F, lines 2306–2364):

real(r8), intent(in) :: landm(pcols)   ! Land fraction ramping to zero over ocean
...
! Ramp between polluted value over land to clean value over ocean.
rel(i,k) = rel(i,k) + (rliqocean-rel(i,k)) * min(1.0_r8,max(0.0_r8,1.0-landm(i)))

So landm is a separate field from landfrac — both are passed into reltab, and only landm is used for the ramp (landfrac is unused in this subroutine; it's a leftover argument). CESM documents it as a "land ocean transition mask: ocean (0), continent (1), transition (0–1)", shipped as the LANDM_COSLAT variable on the CAM boundary-topography file (e.g. USGS-gtopo30_1.9x2.5_remap_*.nc), generated offline by the definesurf/NCL tool chain — the smoothing recipe is not in the model source; the field ships as data.

Our plugin substitutes the pipeline's local land_cov, which is 1.00 across the entire Florida peninsula. That is the deviation.

(Side note from the same source: we also omit CAM's fourth ramp, icefrac → rliqice. Since rliqice = rliqocean = 14, and sea-ice points already have land_cov = 0 so the ocean ramp reaches 14 first, it's a genuine no-op. Fine to leave out.)

Two ways to build it

(a) Use CAM's actual field — no free knobs. Pull LANDM_COSLAT off a public CAM bnd_topo file from the CESM inputdata server and interpolate to our profile lat/lons. This is the disciplined option: it's the same data CAM's 8/14 constants were tuned against, and it invents nothing. Cost: one external dataset, and a resolution mismatch — the field is T42/1.9°×2.5°, we run ~16 km.

(b) Smooth land_cov ourselves. Cheap, self-contained, but introduces exactly the free parameter this repo tries to avoid: the smoothing length. I probed it anyway to size the effect.

What the probe says (read-only, no run)

Great-circle Gaussian on land_cov, applied to the CAM3LIQ run's 89,727 profiles:

σFlorida land land_cov → landmFlorida liquid r_escene liquid layers coarsened / finedmean Δr_e
150 km1.00 → 0.478.0 → 11.4 µm16.5% / 22.9%+0.04 µm
300 km1.00 → 0.298.0 → 12.5 µm17.4% / 33.7%+0.08 µm

It does hit Florida hard and correctly — 8 → 11.4–12.5 µm, which is very close to the 9–10 µm the aerosol-trend argument supports and to the ~11 µm MODIS land climatology, arrived at without tuning anything.

(Caveat: the probe can't reproduce the snow ramp — snow_swe isn't stored in crtm_input.nc. Over snow-covered Quebec the snow ramp has already pushed r_e toward 14, so the smoothing will move those pixels less than the table implies. Florida has zero snow, so the Florida rows are exact.)

The catch, and it's a real one

The smoothing is two-sided. landm is nonzero over near-shore ocean, so it fines coastal marine droplets below 14 — 23–34% of liquid layers in this scene move in that direction, more than are coarsened. The scene-mean Δr_e is essentially zero (+0.04 µm); this is a redistribution, not a shift.

That's the wrong direction for our worst pixels. The 46 cloud_both green offenders in the Florida box are offshore, already at 14 µm, dG +4.76 — they need coarser, and faithful landm smoothing would make them finer. Thread L's standing conclusion is the same: 14 is the low edge of the [14, 23] zero-crossing bracket.

The other surprise: at σ=300 km, median landm over interior-land pixels in this scene is only 0.74 — eastern North America is coastal enough that the smoothing shifts continental cloud from 8 to ~9.6 µm essentially everywhere. That's a scene-wide change, not a Florida patch.

My read

It's a legitimate faithfulness fix and it's cheap, but I'd expect it to be roughly neutral on the metrics — it fixes the land side of a population that is 89% sim_only (a cloud GOES doesn't see) while degrading the ocean side of the population that's actually in cloud_both. If you run it, run variant (a) with the real LANDM_COSLAT, so the result is a statement about CAM rather than about a smoothing length I picked.

Ranked against the alternative: testing a coarser marine value (~20 µm) targets the genuine cloud_both residual directly, and would need a published source to stay within the no-free-knobs rule.
