# CAM6 liquid r_e vs CAM3 — what changed, and what it does here

The CAM6 parametrization (verified verbatim from the CESM2.1 release)

CAM6 has no prescribed liquid r_e at all. Its r_e is a moment diagnostic of the MG2 two-moment microphysics (Gettelman & Morrison 2015; PSD framework Morrison & Gettelman 2008), which assumes a gamma PSD n(D) = N₀ Dᵘ e^(−λD) over the prognostic in-cloud mass q_c and droplet number N_c. From the release code (ESCOMP/CAM cam_cesm2_1_rel, micro_mg_utils.F90 + micro_mg2_0.F90):

! Get pgam from fit to observations of martin et al. 1994   <- stale comment
pgam = 1.0_r8 - 0.7_r8 * exp(-0.008_r8*1.e-6_r8*ncic*rho)   ! eta(Nc)
pgam = 1._r8/(pgam**2) - 1._r8                              ! mu = 1/eta^2 - 1
pgam = max(pgam, 2._r8)
...
props_loc%lambda_bounds = (pgam+1._r8)*1._r8/[50.e-6_r8, 2.e-6_r8]  ! mean diam 2-50 um
...
effc(i,k) = (pgam(i,k)+3._r8)/lamc(i,k)/2._r8*1.e6_r8       ! r_e = (mu+3)/(2*lambda)

with qcic = min(qc/lcldm, 5e-3), and N_c supplied by MAM4 aerosol activation (Abdul-Razzak & Ghan 2000). The dispersion is the Rotstayn & Liu (2003) ε–N relation in its upper-bound form (their λ ∈ {0.001, 0.003, 0.008}; CAM6 uses 0.008 — confirmed via Zhao et al., Atmósfera 2007 which tabulates the three RL03 coefficients). The code comment still says "martin et al. 1994"; the successor ESCOMP/PUMAS repo corrects it: "fit Rotstayn and Liu 2003 (changed from Martin 1994 for CAM6)".

Two useful analytic facts: at equal (LWC, N_c) the RL03 dispersion makes CAM6's r_e ~13 % coarser than the Martin-k relation; and at the nccons default (100 cm⁻³), η=0.686 ⇒ μ clips to 2 everywhere, so operational-fallback CAM6 is effectively a single-shape scheme.

Feasibility and test

ERA5 carries no prognostic N_c, so full CAM6 (aerosol-activated N_c) is not implementable from this data — that's a model-system property, not a borrowable knob. What is faithful: MG2's own documented constant-number mode, which I implemented as CAM6LIQ (plugins/cam6_liquid.py, every constant from the release code, prediction pre-registered in the docstring) and ran in the champion frame:

Verdict: champion-adjacent null. The green patches persist (image confirms). One instructive nuance: CAM6LIQ actually coarsens the offenders' mass-wt r_e (6.1→7.7 µm — the land wing moves 300→100 cm⁻³) yet dG barely moves — reinforcing that this population needs ~14 µm, which no (LWC/N_c)^⅓ law reaches at ERA5's deck LWC (would need N_c ≈ 3–6 cm⁻³).

The punchline for this loop: the CAM lineage moved away from the thing that works here. CAM3's prescribed table fixes the patches precisely because it ignores LWC; CAM5/6's more physical PSD-diagnostic inherits ERA5's low deck LWC and lands where Martin does, regardless of dispersion generation. So "upgrade CAM3→CAM6" is a downgrade for this scene — unless one day the loop gets a defensible N_c and LWC source.

# Error budget of the CAM3LIQ config — where the errors live now

Method: error mass = share of Σ(n·MAE) across cloud-state × surface strata from eval.json; biases read per CTT bin; each attribution backed by a binned diagnostic (diag_coldtops, diag_green, diag_splitwindow, diag_intersection), with per-stratum deltas vs champion to isolate what the liquid swap moved.

Ranked, dominant first

1. Cold-cloud under-opacity (ERA5 ice amount) — ~half of all BT error, liquid-invariant. cloud_both sea+land hold 51 % of scene C14 error mass; CTT-binned C14 bias is +28.7 K (<220 K) / +10.8 (220–240) — identical to champion to ±0.01. The decomposition on this config reproduces the settled result: dHeight −8.8 (model tops are colder than obs — not a height error), dTransp +37.8 (emission from below = under-opacity); the opaque∧overcast cores match to −2.3 K, while thin (τ<3: +39.5) and scattered (TCC<0.7: +54) columns carry the bias. Two "other" signals are the same root seen elsewhere: thin-cloud green (dG +2.24 where dC14 +15.5) and dCO₂ −6.7/−2.4 in cold bins. Settled #1b — needs ice mass, out of scope.

2. Placement / representativeness strata — input data, not microphysics. sim_only sea (n=36 710) is 28.5 % of C14 error mass (−3.2 K; warm/low marine cloud the GOES cold proxy doesn't count) and — notable — 46 % of the scene's R error mass (bias +0.72), making it the largest single R reservoir; it's placement-confounded, not a particle lever. ref_only land (n=1303, pure misses) has the biggest raw biases anywhere: C14 +14.3, C15 +15.8. The colour verdicts are placement-clean: on the sim∩GOES intersection R +0.13/0.90, G +0.30/1.83 ≈ the ref-cloud values.

3. Ice r_e structure (thread D) — the biggest in-scope colour residual now. Cold-cirrus R: sim −1.47 vs ref −1.16 (−0.31 too negative — Wyser's 21 µm cold tops vs the wanted ~36 µm), warm/mid bins overshoot (+0.25/+0.59; the coarser liquid nudged the warm bin by just +0.06). The R cancellation structure survives the liquid swap untouched — the lever remains the ice r_e's IWC/T structure.

4. Land-cloud green +1.2 K (ice-dom over land +1.21) — the residual 8.5 µm emissivity-DB share (bracketed IGBP↔NPOESS ≈0.8 K) plus remainder; now the largest surviving G bias in any big stratum. Needs a spectral atlas (CAMEL/UWiremis).

5. CAM3LIQ's own residuals (new, modest). (a) Liquid-dominated decks still +1.3…+1.6 dG — 14 µm is the low edge of the 14–23 µm zero-crossing; truth nearer ~20 µm. (b) A coarse-side overshoot over snow surfaces: cloud/snow dG flipped +0.29→−0.48, independently corroborated at 3.9 µm (SW cloud/snow −0.50→+1.15; night-valid in this scene) — both signs consistent with slightly-too-coarse liquid where G was already fine. (c) Thick-ice G stays ~−1.0 (ice/ERA5 side, not liquid).

6. Clear-land green −1.20 (IGBP near-grey 8.5 µm DB, settled bracket) — 7.5 % of G error mass, relatively more visible now that cloud G shrank.

Bottom line

After CAM3LIQ the colour target is the healthiest it's been — cloud_both G +0.27/1.82, R +0.10/0.89, C14 +5.24/13.66 — and the in-scope colour error has consolidated into two things: the ice r_e structure (thread D) and the land 8.5 µm emissivity limit. The two much larger BT reservoirs (ice amount, placement) are input-data limits the loop deliberately doesn't tune. The snow-surface overshoot (5b) is the one genuinely new lead from this analysis: two bands agree CAM3's 14 µm is a touch coarse for snow-surface decks while the liquid-dominated decks want coarser than 14 — a tension a single prescribed table can't close, worth remembering when judging promotion.
